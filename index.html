Almost everything here comes from the original church and civil registers, read in October 2026.

    From Swiss Reformed to Alsatian Lutheran. Peter Barth was one of many Swiss who moved into Alsace after the Thirty Years' War left its villages half empty. He belonged to Bern's Reformed church, while Quatzenheim had been Lutheran since 1541 and had no other church. His son Jacob stayed Reformed all his life. Jacob's children were baptised by the Lutheran pastor, and by the 1760s the family was simply Lutheran. They stayed Lutheran in Iowa and Minnesota. German, Swiss then Alsatian, was the family's language for two hundred years, until English took over in the generation born in America.

    The places on the map

    What the records tell us about each place, in the order the family came to them.

    • Detligen

      Parish of Radelfingen, near Aarberg, canton Bern

      Home of Peter Barth (generation 1) and birthplace of his son Peter (generation 2). The registers call the son "of Detligen, Bernese territory" in 1687 and again in 1688. Radelfingen's own registers before 1831 are lost.

    • Rothrist (then Niederwil)

      Then Bernese territory

      Home of Ulrich Jäggi, whose daughter Barbara Jäggi married Peter Barth in 1687. Swiss Reformed.

    • Strasbourg

      The Reformed church of Strasbourg

      Peter Barth married in 1687 and had his children baptised in the Reformed church of Strasbourg. His son Jacob was baptised there, in the Reformed faith, though Quatzenheim's own church was Lutheran.

    • Quatzenheim

      A farming village outside Strasbourg, Alsace

      Peter Barth, tailor and burgher, had settled here by 1687. The line lived here for two hundred years, from Peter to Jean (generations 2 to 7). The village had been Lutheran since 1541 and had no other church. Catharina Hans, Jacob's wife, was born here in 1687, and Anna Rohfritsch, the younger Valentin's wife, was of Quatzenheim.

    • Wolfisheim

      Near Strasbourg

      Birthplace in 1775 of Anne Scheer (Scherer), who married Michel Barth in 1804.

    • Schwindratzheim

      Alsace

      Birthplace in 1813 of Marie Wendling, who married Jean Barth in 1833, and of their son John (Jean) Barth, born in 1833.

    • Stratford

      Ontario, about 1854

      John Barth left Alsace about 1854 and came by way of Ontario on his way to Iowa. The ports and the ships are not known.

    • Locust

      Winneshiek County, Iowa, from 1855

      John Barth farmed here from 1855 and was buried from the Lutheran church at Locust. His son Charles (Carl) was born here in 1874.

    • Decorah

      St John's, the German Lutheran church

      John's sons were confirmed at St John's. Charles was confirmed in 1890, in a church register kept in German.

    • Preston

      Fillmore County, Minnesota, by 1900

      Charles Barth farmed here.

    The wives

    Each of these women married into the line. What is known of where they came from:

    • Barbara Jäggi, married Peter 1687. Daughter of Ulrich Jäggi of Niederwil (now Rothrist), then Bernese territory. Swiss Reformed.
    • Catharina Hans, married Jacob 1716. Born 1687 at Quatzenheim, daughter of Jacob Hans and Gertrud.
    • Catharina Martzloff, Jacob's second wife, 1730. Daughter of Michael Martzloff, farmer, and Brigitta.
    • Margaretha Schwebel, married Valentin 1743. Daughter of Michael Schwebel, farmer, of a village not yet read with certainty.
    • Anna Rohfritsch, married Valentin 1765. Of Quatzenheim, daughter of Andreas Rohfritsch and Anna Brumter.
    • Anne Scheer (Scherer), married Michel 1804. Born 1775 at Wolfisheim, near Strasbourg.
    • Marie Wendling, married Jean 1833. Born 1813 at Schwindratzheim.
    • Margaretha Matter, married John 1860. Born 1842 at Ingenheim, Alsace, per the 1996 Winneshiek County family history.
    • Louise Anna Kern, married Charles 1898. Born in Minnesota in 1878 to German-speaking parents.
